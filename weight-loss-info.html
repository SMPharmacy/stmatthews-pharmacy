What is medical weight management?

Medical weight management is a clinician-led approach to losing weight, where a healthcare professional assesses your health, agrees a realistic plan with you and — where it is appropriate — prescribes a licensed medicine to support your efforts. It is not a diet product bought off a shelf, and it is not a quick fix. It is a structured programme with proper assessment and follow-up.

Over-the-counter diet aids (such as fat binders or appetite suppressants sold without a prescription) work in limited ways and are not individually assessed for you. A prescription GLP-1 medication like Mounjaro works differently: it acts on the appetite-regulating signals in the body to help you feel fuller and eat less. Because it is a prescription-only medicine, it can only be supplied after a consultation and where a pharmacist judges it clinically suitable for you.

Our medical weight management service in Leicester is led by a GPhC-registered pharmacist. Medication, when used, is always one part of a wider plan built around diet, physical activity and lasting habit change — never a replacement for them.

Treatment options

Medications we offer

Mounjaro is a prescription-only GLP-1 medicine, offered only after a consultation and where clinically appropriate.

Mounjaro Tirzepatide

What it is: a once-weekly injection containing tirzepatide, a prescription-only medicine licensed for weight management in eligible adults.

How it works: it acts on two natural gut hormone receptors (GIP and GLP-1) that influence appetite, helping you feel fuller for longer and eat less.

Typical results: when combined with a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity, it may help support weight loss. Results vary from person to person, and a consultation is needed to see whether it is right for you.

Common side effects: nausea, diarrhoea, constipation, indigestion and tiredness, which often ease as your body adjusts. We start at a low dose and increase gradually, and the pharmacist will talk you through these before you start.

Prescription-only medicines

Mounjaro is a prescription-only medicine. It is not suitable for everyone and is only supplied after a consultation, and only where the pharmacist judges treatment to be clinically appropriate for you.

Eligibility

Who is this service for?

The clearest way to know is to book a consultation — the pharmacist will confirm whether treatment is suitable for you.

This service may suit you if…

  • You are an adult (18 or over)
  • You have a BMI of 30 or above, or
  • You have a BMI of 27 or above and a weight-related health condition (such as high blood pressure, type 2 diabetes or high cholesterol)
  • You are ready to make lifestyle changes alongside treatment

This service is not for you if…

  • You are pregnant, breastfeeding or trying to conceive
  • You are under 18
  • You have certain medical conditions or a history of specific illnesses (the pharmacist will check this with you)
  • You take medicines that may not be compatible with treatment

Not sure which applies to you? That's exactly what the consultation is for — to check your suitability.

What to expect during treatment

Weight loss with these medicines is gradual, not sudden. Most people who respond notice changes over the course of around three to six months, with the dose increased slowly over that time. Everyone is different, and the pace and amount of weight change varies from person to person.

Lifestyle changes matter. The medicine supports your efforts — it does not do the work on its own. Eating well, moving more and building sustainable habits are what make the results last, which is why our programme includes ongoing support rather than just a prescription.

Managing side effects. Side effects such as nausea are most common in the early weeks and usually ease as your body adjusts. Starting low and increasing the dose gradually helps. We'll give you practical advice on managing them and review how you're getting on at each check-in.

When to contact us. Contact the pharmacist if side effects are severe or persistent, if you feel unwell, or if you have any questions about your treatment. In a medical emergency, always call 999.
