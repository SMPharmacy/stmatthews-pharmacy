NHS Pharmacy First

Urinary Tract Infection

A urinary tract infection (UTI) affects the bladder and the tubes that carry urine. A pharmacist can assess an uncomplicated lower UTI and supply antibiotics where appropriate under NHS Pharmacy First.

Book a consultation ← All Pharmacy First conditions

Who this is for

Women aged 16 to 64. If you fall outside this, we can still give advice and point you to the right care.

About urinary tract infection

This part of the service is for women aged 16 to 64 with an uncomplicated lower UTI. Some symptoms and situations need a doctor instead, and the pharmacist will always check for these first.

Symptoms and what to look out for

Common signs include:

  • Pain or a burning feeling when you pee
  • Needing to pee more often or more urgently than usual
  • Needing to pee during the night
  • Cloudy, dark or strong-smelling pee
  • Pain low down in your tummy
  • Generally feeling unwell or achy

Where your symptoms fit an uncomplicated lower UTI, the pharmacist can supply antibiotics under the NHS protocol. Drinking enough fluids and using pain relief also help while you recover.

When to get help urgently

Seek urgent help

This service is not suitable for everyone. Please tell us — or see your GP — if any of these apply, as you may need different care:

  • You are pregnant, or think you might be
  • You have a high temperature, shivering, or pain in your side or lower back
  • There is blood in your pee
  • You are male, under 16 or over 64, or have a urinary catheter
  • You get UTIs often, or symptoms do not improve within 48 hours of treatment

A high temperature with back or side pain can mean a kidney infection — call NHS 111 for advice.

What to expect from the consultation

You will be seen in our private consultation room — a room with a door, not the shop floor. The consultation itself is always free.

  • The pharmacist will ask about your symptoms in the private consultation room.
  • They will check the infection is an uncomplicated one suitable for the service.
  • Where appropriate, antibiotics are supplied there and then, with self-care advice.
  • If your symptoms suggest something more, we will refer you to a GP.

If we supply a medicine and you pay for prescriptions, the standard NHS prescription charge applies — the same as at your GP. If you are exempt from prescription charges, it is free.
